Compost Made Simple

A practical, beginner-friendly plan for a small website that helps people turn everyday food scraps into useful compost.

Site Name

Compost Made Simple tells new composters exactly what the site offers: clear, approachable help without requiring prior gardening experience. The sprout logo represents food scraps becoming healthy soil.

Possible domain: compostmadesimple.org (The domain is currently available.)

Site Purpose

The site will help households start and maintain a home compost system. It will explain how to choose a bin, balance “greens” and “browns,” and use finished compost. A troubleshooting guide and question form will help visitors solve common problems such as odors, pests, and slow decomposition.

Scenarios

  • What food scraps can I put in a beginner compost bin?
  • How do I balance green materials with dry brown materials?
  • Which compost bin will work in a small apartment or yard?
  • Why does my compost smell bad, and how can I fix it?

Planned Pages and Content

Home

A short introduction, a three-step overview, and links to the starter guide and troubleshooting advice.

Getting Started

Bin options, a green-and-brown materials guide, and a simple routine for adding and turning compost.

Troubleshooting

Common symptoms with practical fixes, plus a form for visitors to submit a composting question.

Planned form: The Troubleshooting page will include labeled name, email, topic, and question fields. Required fields, email input validation, clear instructions, and a confirmation message will support usability and accessibility.

Color Schema

The palette uses soil and plant colors to make the guide feel natural, calm, and easy to scan.

  • Forest green#2F5D3AHeader, footer, and section headings
  • Warm cream#FAF7EFPage background and open content areas
  • Terracotta#A84F1FButtons, links, and small highlights
  • Soil charcoal#24301FBody text for strong contrast

Accessibility and Functionality

  • Responsive layouts will stack on narrow screens and use wider grids on desktop.
  • Semantic headings, labeled form fields, keyboard-friendly controls, and visible focus styles will support accessible use.
  • JavaScript will use an array of compost materials to filter items into greens and browns, with conditional guidance based on the visitor’s choices.
  • The form will provide clear validation and a confirmation message. Local storage will remember the visitor’s preferred compost setup on the device.
  • Content images will be compressed and use descriptive alternative text and native lazy loading.
