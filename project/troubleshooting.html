Troubleshooting

Small changes can bring a compost pile back into balance.

Compost changes with the weather and the materials you add. Open a symptom below for a practical first adjustment.

Common compost problems

The pile smells sour or rotten

Too much moisture or too little air is a common cause. Mix in dry leaves or torn cardboard, then loosen compacted layers. Bury new food scraps in the center.

Flies or other pests are visiting

Cover exposed food scraps with a layer of browns and keep the lid closed. Avoid meat, dairy, oils, and pet waste in a typical backyard bin.

The material is very dry

Add a little water while mixing until the material feels like a wrung-out sponge. Include fresh greens in small amounts and check again after a few days.

Nothing seems to be breaking down

Small pieces decompose faster than large ones. Chop scraps, check that the pile is slightly damp, and add some greens if the mix is mostly dry leaves or cardboard.

Safety note: If you are unsure whether a material is safe for your compost setup, leave it out and check the guidance from your local compost program.
